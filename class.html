Time Machine for teachers

Walk ancient Rome (and Old Delhi, Cancún and Beijing) in 3D, in the browser. Make a link, give it to the class: students open it and play at once.

1. Make your class link

Students see "your teacher can see your progress" at the bottom of their screen. What is sent: the name they chose for their character, the city, their money, the sights found, a few counts and short lines like "found the Pantheon". Never chat and never anything typed. It is kept in memory on the game server for at most ten hours and never saved. If you teach children, check that your school allows this before you turn it on.

📊 Open the class dashboard

Your class is tied to your account, so the dashboard shows the same class on any computer once you log in. "Start a new class" makes a fresh class, so students of the old link are no longer shown.

Scavenger hunt (optional: tick the sights the students must find. A checklist shows on their screen, and when they have found them all they get a finished screen with their name, the time and a code to show you.)

Or give the class a code (they type it into the box on the front page, "Have a class code?"; the class name is not in the code)

Works without Wi-Fi

  • Patchy Wi-Fi is fine. The first time a computer opens the game with the internet on, it saves the game in the browser. After that the game opens and plays with the Wi-Fi off or dropping. Open your class link once on each computer beforehand (the day before is best). Only the dashboard and playing against other people need the internet.
  • No Wi-Fi at all, or nothing installed? Download the offline copy (zip, 1 MB), put it on a USB stick or a shared drive, unzip it and open start.html. It needs no internet, not even once. It has the Latin names, the hunt and the shops; it has no dashboard and no class codes.
  • Slow computer? Tick Light graphics above. The game also switches to it by itself when it runs slowly.

2. What students get

  • No login. They open the link and make a character (a name and a class).
  • Nothing is saved. Each student starts fresh, and the game keeps nothing on the computer after the tab is closed.
  • No other players, no chat. Class mode is offline: nobody from outside the class can appear.
  • No time limit and no limit on denarii (the game's play money).
  • Nothing leaves the computer. In class mode the game sends nothing to our server: no names, no activity. Character names are not checked, so you may want to tell students to use a made-up name.
  • School-friendly. No betting at the tables and no breaking into houses.
  • Works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on a laptop. The game needs a keyboard and mouse.

3. Controls

W A S Dwalk (hold Shift to run)
mouse (drag), or Q Elook around
Cchange the camera (first person, behind you, from above)
Kswitch between ancient and modern
Gguide me to the nearest sight I have not seen
Ftalk to a shopkeeper, sit on a stool, press the button at a place
Mthe map

Walk up to a shop door and it opens by itself. Every shop has a keeper to talk to (F), who sells, buys, gives errands and tells you where the nearest sight is.

4. Ideas for a lesson

  • Find the Forum, the Pantheon and the Colosseum. Read the Pantheon's inscription (M·AGRIPPA·L·F·COS·TERTIVM·FECIT) and translate it.
  • With Latin names on, walk into the shops and read what the keepers say. Translate each line, then check it against the English.
  • Press K at the same spot to compare the ancient city and today's.
  • Find the places named in a text the class is reading (the Rostra, the Temple of Vesta, the Circus Maximus).

The Latin in the game is the author's own: please check it before you use it with a class, and tell us what to correct.
