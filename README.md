# Walk Ancient Rome

A walkable 3D Ancient Rome, seen at street level, as it stood around 128 AD under Hadrian. Explore it like Street View, discover 17 landmarks and read the history behind each one.

Built with plain HTML/JS and [Three.js](https://threejs.org) (loaded from a CDN). No build step, no install.

## Play

Serve the folder and open it in a browser:

```bash
ruby -run -e httpd . -p 8123
# or: python3 -m http.server 8123
```

Then visit http://localhost:8123. Because it is static, it also works on GitHub Pages as-is.

## Controls

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Walk / strafe | Arrow keys or `W` `A` `S` `D` |
| Run | `Shift` |
| Look | Drag the mouse (double-click to lock the mouse) |
| Turn | `Q` `E` |
| Map (click to teleport) | `M` |
| List of places | `P` |
| Time of day | `T` |
| Fight (in the Colosseum arena) | `F` to start. `Space` or click attacks, hold `Shift` or right-click blocks, `WASD` moves, `Esc` leaves |
| Animal hunt (in the arena) | `U`. Bow and arrows: hold `Space` or the mouse to draw, release to shoot, `Shift` dodge roll, `WASD` move |
| Lion (in the arena) | `L`. A lion on its own, hunted with the bow (same controls as the animal hunt) |
| Naval battle (in the arena) | `N`. The arena floods and you command a galley. `W` `S` sail, `A` `D` steer, `Q` / `E` fire port / starboard cannons, `Space` or click fires at the nearest enemy |
| Watch (Colosseum or Circus stands) | `V` to start or stop. In the Colosseum it shows the day's programme: `N` skips to the next act, `1`-`5` picks one. `C` switches to a cinematic camera, `WASD` lets you walk around the stands |
| Race (on the Circus Maximus track) | `R` to start. `W` gas, `S` brake, `A` `D` steer (hold `Shift` for fine steering), `Q` `E` hop a lane, `1`-`9` speed level (1 slowest, 9 gallop), `Space` whip, `X` cruise control, `C` cycle camera, hold `B` to look back, `Esc` leaves |

## What you can do

- Walk the Via Sacra through the Forum, up the steps of the temples, and into the Colosseum (climb the seats) and the Pantheon
- Discover landmarks by walking near them. Progress is saved in your browser
- Jump anywhere from the map or the Places list
- **Old Delhi:** press `B` (or the ✈ Travel button) to travel to Shahjahanabad, Shah Jahan's walled capital, and back to Rome. Walk Chandni Chowk (a canal down the middle, the moonlit pool, shops and bazaars on both sides) from the Fatehpuri Masjid to the Lahori Gate of the Red Fort, then through Chhatta Chowk and the Naubat Khana to the Diwan-i-Aam, the marble Diwan-i-Khas and Rang Mahal beside the Stream of Paradise, and the Muthamman Burj over the river wall. South of the avenue the Jama Masjid stands on its rock with flights of steps up to the courtyard, and you have a plot of your own off Chandni Chowk: you start with a small one-room house, and the Market sells a house with a garden (150), a grand haveli (400, rent 15 a morning) and a mahal with a tower (1,000, rent 40), each healing you faster at home. Rome and Delhi each keep their own house. `K` switches between **Mughal Delhi, c. 1650** (elephants and bullock carts on the avenue, the canal, Begum ki Serai, the fort's gardens) and **Modern Delhi** (the Town Hall, cycle rickshaws, the Chandni Chowk Metro entrance, barracks in the fort, the flag over the Lahori Gate). 11 sights to discover (14 in Modern Delhi: the Metro station, **India Gate** and **Kartavya Path**). A long road runs south from the Jama Masjid lane to New Delhi: in Modern Delhi it ends at India Gate (a 42 m sandstone arch with the names carved on its walls, three granite steps, the canopy with the statue behind it, and the ceremonial avenue between its two canals and lawns); in Shah Jahan's day the same ground is open scrubland. **Games of Old Delhi (`N` in Delhi):** a race down Chandni Chowk (rickshaws in Modern Delhi, elephants in Mughal Delhi, against three rivals and the street traffic, with a boost, nine speed levels on keys `1`-`9` and cruise on `X`: the higher the level, the faster you tire, so pace yourself), *Defend the Red Fort* (aim a cannon on the Lahori Gate at three waves of raiders), *kushti* wrestling in a mud pit (take hold, then mash Space to throw; first to two throws), *kite fights* (fly your kite across the others' strings and hold Space to saw through them), a *chaat run* (carry hot food to three customers through the crowds before it cools or spills), *pachisi* (the Mughal board game of cowrie shells, against a rival) and a *bazaar trader* game (buy cheap in one bazaar and sell dear in another over six days). Each game has its own place in the city (the akhara, the race arches, a cannon on the fort wall, a kite stall, a pachisi pavilion, a bazaar stall and a chaat stall): walk up and press `F`, or find them in Places and Directions (`G`), where they appear after the sights. All single-player for now. The fights, races and naval battles stay in Rome. The places and facts come from the history of the sites; building shapes are simplified and not to scale between districts
- **Exploring pays:** every new sight you discover, in Rome or Delhi, pays 50 denarii (once per sight; your house and the game spots do not count).
- **Other players:** when the game server is reachable, other people walking around the same city appear near you as named characters (`👥 n other players nearby` under your stats). They are only for show: you can't touch or hurt each other outside the online games. They also see your clothes and your companion: a hound trotting behind you or an eagle circling above. Your vehicle too: they see you riding a car, scooter, rickshaw, cart or elephant, and they see the one you left parked. You only see people within about 260 m, up to 30 at a time, in the same city (Rome or Delhi).
- **Weather and seasons:** a year is 28 days (seven per season: spring, summer, autumn and winter, which Delhi calls spring, summer, monsoon and winter). The weather changes every few game hours: clear, cloudy, rain, thunderstorms (flashes and thunder) or fog that closes in around you. The odds depend on the season and the city: Rome has damp winters and dry summers, Delhi has a monsoon and thick winter fog. The sky greys, rain falls and hisses, and the grass and leaves change colour with the season. A badge next to the era shows the weather and season. It is worked out from the day and hour, so it needs no server.
- **Chat:** press `Enter` (or the 💬 Chat button) to talk to everyone who is online, `Enter` again to send, `Esc` to close. `/w name message` whispers to one person (names with spaces work), `/help` lists the commands. The server cleans messages, limits how fast you can send them and masks swearing; new people see the last 40 messages. **Admin:** accounts listed in the `admins` table (see `supabase-admin.sql`) get a 🛠 Admin button with the list of who has signed up (email, character, when they joined and last logged in) and who is online, with Mute and Kick buttons, plus the chat commands `/who`, `/kick name`, `/mute name [minutes]`, `/unmute name` and `/say message`. **Forgot your password?** There is a button on the log-in screen that emails a reset link (set the Site URL in Supabase, Authentication > URL Configuration).
- **Directions:** click the big yellow **Directions** button (or press `G`) and pick a place. You get a walking route around the buildings, drawn on the minimap and map, gold markers on the ground, a light beam at the destination and a card with the distance, walking time and turn-by-turn text. `G` also opens the panel; there is a "nearest place I haven't seen" shortcut. Click a pin on the map for directions too
- **Sound:** everything is synthesised, no audio files. Places make sound you can hear from a distance (the crowds of the Colosseum and the Circus, market chatter in the Forum, birds and wind on the hills, water in the naval battle). It gets louder as you approach, pans to the side it comes from and is muffled when it is behind you. Footsteps change with the ground, and there are effects for swords, roars, cannons, hooves and more. `O` turns sound on and off
- **Crashes:** run into a building, a wall or a column and you crash: the screen flashes and shakes, you stagger, you are knocked back a step and you lose some health (more if you were running). Run into a citizen and you knock them over. The Circus Maximus has a podium wall between the track and the seats (use the stair entrances to climb up) and a tall wall behind the stands, so running into them hurts too. Walking into things is only a gentle bump. **Days and nights:** a day lasts 20 minutes (50 seconds per hour) with a real sunrise, sunset, moon and stars; the clock is at the top. After dark, go to Your House (near the Colosseum road, in Places and Directions), step inside and press Z to sleep: you wake at dawn fully healed and the horses gain a little. Stay awake until 4 am and you collapse, wake in your bed and lose 15 denarii. T skips ahead in the day, but not through the night (it stops at 4 am and you collapse); from 10:30 pm the clock warns you that you are sleepy. **Touch controls:** on a phone or tablet, a joystick (bottom-left) drives movement, dragging anywhere else looks around, and Jump/Run buttons appear bottom-right. Fights, races and naval battles get their own on-screen Attack/Block, Whip/Lane-hop/Cruise, or Fire buttons. Desktop keyboard and mouse are unaffected. **Character and class:** the first time you enter Rome you choose a name, a class (Gladiator, Charioteer, Venator or Merchant, each with bonuses and a weakness) and your look (skin, hair, toga, sash). Press Y to change it later. **Ancient Rome / Modern Rome:** press K (or the toolbar button) to switch the whole city between 128 AD and today. Modern Rome is shown as it looks now: the Colosseum keeps its tall wall on the north side and has collapsed to a low stub on the south; temple and basilica roofs are gone and many columns are broken or missing (and no longer block your way); the paving is cracked, with missing slabs and weeds, and the Colosseum arena floor is gone, showing the chambers beneath; the Circus Maximus is a grass park; the Colossus is gone; apartment blocks have rows of windows; asphalt streets with lane markings run between them, with parked cars and street lamps you can crash into (the lamps glow at night); and there is moving traffic: cars drive the streets, brake for each other and for you, honk, and hit you if you step in front of one at speed. Traffic hum replaces the crowd noise. Ruined parts of the Colosseum and columns are real in the physics too: only things that stand today still block you. The Colosseum, Forum and other landmarks weather into ruins (or, for the Pantheon and a few others, stay much the same), the Circus Maximus becomes a grass park, the Colossus disappears (it was melted down centuries ago), apartment blocks and citizens switch to a modern look, and the Colosseum/Circus games only work in Ancient Rome. Landmark cards show a green 'Today'' note in Modern Rome. **Team battle (play online):** 🌐 Play online > *Team up: beat 5 bots together*. Up to four players fight side by side against five enemy gladiator bots, all refereed by the game server. A fallen player is revived if a teammate stands next to them for three seconds; the team wins when all five bots are down and loses if everyone is down. Tick the bots box and friendly ally bots fill a small team up to three (so you can play it alone). **Cars (Modern Rome only):** in the Market, buy a Vespa scooter (250), a city car (500) or a sports car (1,200), take it out, walk up and press X to get in. W gas, S brake and reverse, A/D steer, Space handbrake, Esc or X to get out. You can drive anywhere the world lets you; crashes cost health, pedestrians you hit are knocked over, and moving traffic brakes for you. **Rickshaws, carts and elephants:** the Market also sells a cycle rickshaw (200, modern cities), a bullock cart (150) and an elephant (800, with a red and gold howdah; ancient cities: Ancient Rome and Mughal Delhi). Take it out, walk up and press X to climb on; they handle like the cars but slower (rickshaw 25 km/h, cart 18, elephant 50), and they swing their legs as they walk. Switching era puts away whatever does not exist in the new one. **The Market (denarii):** the 🛒 Market button lets you spend the denarii you win. Upgrade your house in three steps: a garden (150: walled garden, fountain, hedges, cypress trees), a grand domus (400: two wings, a colonnade, a statue) and a villa (1,000: a tower, a pool and a gilded pavilion). Better houses heal you faster when you rest at home (1.5x, 2x, 3x), and the grand domus and villa pay you rent each morning (15 and 40 denarii). To wear: laurel wreath 60, crested helmet 120, golden crown 250, crimson cloak 90, royal purple cloak 140, gilded breastplate 180, gold armbands 70, hobnailed boots 50, round shield 110 (other players see them online). Companions that follow you (other players see them too): a hound 150 and an eagle that circles above you 220. House decorations: door banners 60 and a mosaic floor 90. Supplies you can buy again and again: a healing salve 40 (+40 health) and fodder for the horses 40 (+40 fitness). Permanent upgrades for single-player games: well-bred horses 350 (horses tire 15% slower) and gladiator training 400 (sword hits 10% harder against the arena gladiators; not in duels against real people). What you own is saved with your game and your account. **Jump:** press Space to hop. **Camera:** you start in a close third-person view so you can see your own character walk around. Press C to cycle the views (close behind, far behind, side, front, bird's-eye, first person; Shift+C goes back) and scroll the mouse wheel to zoom; your choice is remembered. **Walking speed:** keys 1-9 set how fast you walk (1 slow stroll, 4 normal, 9 sprint); Shift still runs. **Scraping:** grinding along the side of a house or wall slows you down and drains health for as long as you keep scraping (faster = more damage), so step away from it
- **Voice:** a narrator reads captions aloud using your browser's built-in text-to-speech (nothing is downloaded or sent anywhere): the subtitles of the Colosseum shows, the history card of each landmark you reach, the result screens and the turn-by-turn directions. Press `J` or the Voice button to turn it on or off
- **Rest and heal:** you can't play forever. Your health and your racing horses' stamina carry over between games and recover slowly on their own whenever you are not in a game (about 3.5 minutes to get from 20 to full health just by walking around). Press `Z` to sit and rest (move to stand up): that is about four times faster, and the horses only recover quickly at the Circus stables. Fights, hunts and naval battles need health 35 or more, and races need rested horses.
- **Trial by combat:** knock over too many citizens in Ancient Rome (seven within about two and a half minutes, on foot or in a car) and the guards seize you and drag you to the arena, where you must beat four gladiators and then a lion, all with the sword, to win your freedom. There is no prize, you cannot leave until it is over, and losing means dying. You get a warning after three knock-downs and another after five.
- **Dying:** if your health reaches zero (a bad crash, being run over, grinding along walls) you die: the screen goes black, you are carried to your house (in Rome or Delhi) and must stay there for a whole day while the sky runs through it. You wake at the same hour the next day with 55 health, the doctor takes up to 25 denarii and your tenants pay their rent. Below 14 health the game warns you. You can also be killed in the arena: losing a gladiator fight, a lion or animal hunt or the naval battle (your ship sinks) is fatal, and so is dying in a chariot crash. Crashes and cars now hurt about twice as much, so it is easier to die. Online duels against real people are not fatal.
- **Stakes:** you start with 100 denarii. Races cost 20 to enter and pay 100 for 1st and 40 for 2nd. Beating opponents in the arena and the naval battle earns purses. A defeat costs you money and leaves you badly wounded, so it really is possible to lose. Everything is saved in your browser
- Switch between morning, midday and sunset
- **Fight as a gladiator:** stand in the Colosseum arena and press `F` for a three-round duel against a Murmillo, a Thraex and a Champion. Watch for the glow and raised arm that warn of a strike, block it, or step out of range. Stamina limits how often you can swing
- **Race a chariot:** stand on the Circus Maximus track and press `R` for a three-lap race against the Blues, Reds and Whites. You choose one of nine speed levels with the number keys `1`-`9` and must manage the horses' stamina: the top levels tire them, and exhausted horses can only manage a fast trot until they recover. Corners limit your speed, and wide lanes let you carry more of it. The rivals' horses get tired too: they gallop while fresh, slow down as they wear out and ease off to recover (the standings list shows "(resting)"), so a steady pace beats a burst of speed. The rivals are tougher than they look: they pick good lanes and two out of three will cut across to block you, the tightest inside lane is slow, and if a rival crosses the line first the race is over and you lose. Collisions cost you: bumping another chariot (or scraping the spina or the stands) knocks your speed down and slows the horses for a couple of seconds. Hop between lanes with `Q` and `E`, use the whip for short bursts, and switch between four camera views (chase, high, driver, overhead)
- **Lion hunt:** stand in the arena and press `L` to hunt a single lion with the bow. It crouches, then pounces, so keep your distance and use the dodge roll
- **Naval battle:** press `N` in the arena. The Colosseum floods, and you command a galley with three cannons per side against two waves of enemy ships. Sail so your broadside faces the enemy, ram them if you dare, and keep away from their guns. (Cannons are a game invention. Real Roman naval fights used rams, boarding and catapults)
- **Animal hunt (bow and arrows):** stand in the arena and press `U` to hunt a wild boar, a bear and then a lion with a bow. Hold `Space` or the mouse to draw, release to shoot: a full draw is strong and accurate, a quick draw is weak and wobbly, and headshots do extra damage. The aim helps by turning toward the animal and leading a moving target, but you still have to time your shots. `Shift` is a dodge roll that lets you slip past a charge. You get a quiver of 30 arrows for each animal in the hunt (14 for the lone lion) and pick up a few spent ones over time. Boars charge fast, bears hit hard and slowly
- **Watch the games:** press `V` in the Colosseum to watch a programme like the real games. It starts with a *venatio* (animal hunt among staged trees and rocks, with hunters, deer and ostriches), then three staged myth dramas (Orpheus charming the beasts, Daedalus and Icarus flying over the arena, Hercules wrestling the Nemean Lion), then gladiator matches (the first is Priscus vs Verus, who really did fight to a draw at the opening games). `N` skips to the next act, `1`-`5` picks one, `C` switches to a cinematic camera. In the Circus stands, `V` shows a chariot race instead
- Crowds of spectators fill the stands and cheer while you play

## Security

The game is a static site: no server code, no accounts, no database and no network requests, so there is nothing to break into on the server side. What is protected:

- **Content-Security-Policy** (in the page, and as real headers via `_headers` / `vercel.json`): only the game's own scripts and the one pinned 3D library may run. Injected inline scripts, inline event handlers and scripts from other sites are blocked.
- **Subresource Integrity** on the Three.js CDN script: if the CDN copy ever differs from the exact file checked in `index.html`, the browser refuses to run it.
- Other headers: no framing (clickjacking), no MIME sniffing, no referrer, no camera / microphone / location.
- Saved progress in the browser is validated on load (unknown landmarks dropped, health and money clamped).
- No secrets are stored in the repo.

**What cannot be prevented:** because the game runs in the player's own browser, a determined player can change their own health, money or position with the browser developer tools. That only affects their copy. If you ever add a shared leaderboard or prizes, scores must be checked on a server.

**Keep your accounts safe:** turn on two-factor authentication on GitHub and on the host, use a GitHub token limited to this one repo with a short expiry, and revoke tokens you no longer use.

## Accounts and playing real people

Built and tested: accounts (sign up, login, your game saved online) and live duels and races against real people judged by the game server, with practice bots when nobody is around. Everything stays switched off until you fill in `config.js` and run the server: see `server/README.md` for the step-by-step.
